The House

An independent studio for formed earth. Established at the age of six.

I.

Origins

A garden wall, a borrowed spoon.

The first MudPie was formed in late spring, on the low wall behind a terraced house, and left to dry beside a watering can. It was not sold. It was not, strictly speaking, finished: a sibling sat on it before the curing was complete. The studio still regards this as its first review.

Within a summer there were more. They were arranged along the wall in order of completion and given names, which were written beneath them in chalk and erased by the next rain. In this way the founder learned, at six, the two lessons on which the house is built.

The spoon was borrowed from the kitchen drawer and returned, with apologies, a considerable number of years later.

A thing made carefully deserves a name. The weather will eventually have its say.

II.

Philosophy

A humble material, taken seriously.

We do not believe in improving the earth. We believe in arranging it, briefly, into a shape it will remember for a while.

The pie is the oldest form most people ever make with their hands. It is round because hands are; it is shallow because patience is finite; it is a pie, rather than a bowl or a brick, because that is what children call it and we have never found a better word. We have tried. There was a period, preserved in the archive, when the studio referred to its work as “formed earth objects”. Nobody remembers it fondly.

Our work is to give this ordinary form the attention usually reserved for rare things: provenance, measurement, a name, a record of its life. The form, it turns out, can bear it.

  1. I.

    Nothing is fired.

    Heat would make the pieces permanent, and permanence is not what we are offering.

  2. II.

    Nothing is hurried.

    A piece takes as long as it takes. The studio has missed deadlines it set for itself and regards this as a sign of integrity.

  3. III.

    Nothing is eaten.

    This has never been a problem in the studio. We mention it for completeness.

III.

Method

Four rituals, observed without exception.

  1. 01

    The Retrieval

    Material is collected on foot from beds the studio has visited for years, in quantities small enough that the beds do not notice.

  2. 02

    The Library

    Everything retrieved is dried, sieved three times and sorted by hand into nine earths, each with a name and a temperament.

  3. 03

    The Sitting

    One piece, one sitting, one pair of hands. If the maker stands up, the piece goes back to the library.

  4. 04

    The Cure

    At 2,940 metres, unwrapped, turned once a day at noon, for as long as it needs and not a day longer.

IV.

The Mark

Drawn once, with one finger.

The studio’s mark was drawn when the founder was six, with an index finger dipped in Valley Red, on the back of a seed packet. It is a letter M inside a square, made in a single stroke.

It has never been redrawn, digitised into anything cleaner, or improved upon by a consultant, though one did ask. Today it appears in one place only: pressed into the certificate of formation that accompanies every piece.

In everyday use the house signs itself with a simpler disc — a pressed circle, two rings, one fissure. It was designed in an afternoon by someone who was not six, and it shows.

V.

Earth & Return

Every piece is borrowed.

The earth is taken from beds we have visited for years, in quantities small enough that the beds do not notice, and every piece eventually goes back — by rain, by accident, or by arrangement.

When a piece returns to the earth it is recorded in the catalogue with the same care as a sale, and arguably more. No. 031, Low Tide is the most recent example. The studio does not consider this a loss. It considers it the end of the loan.

We make no claims about the earth that the earth would not make about itself. We hold no certificates beyond our own, and ours, as you may have gathered, are largely ceremonial.

Every piece is eventually returned to the earth. This is not a policy; it is a forecast.

VI.

The Studio

A former dairy, a long table, a door that does not quite close.

The studio occupies a former dairy at the edge of the Red Country, 1,610 metres above sea level, with a north light and a table long enough to hold a whole collection at once.

It is kept by four people, who prefer to be known by what they do. Visitors are welcome on days without rain, which is to say rarely, and with notice.

  • The Maker

    Forms every piece, in one sitting, and does not discuss it afterwards.

  • The Keeper of Earths

    Maintains the library, the sieves and a running argument about Husk.

  • The Weather Correspondent

    Reads the sky, and is consulted before every decision of consequence.

  • The Archivist

    Writes all of this down, including this sentence.

VII.

Chronology

An abridged history, in the order it happened.

  1. Age 6The first pie, on a garden wall. Unsigned; sat upon.
  2. Age 9A second earth is introduced. The household is not consulted.
  3. Age 14A crack is kept on purpose for the first time. It is still in the archive.
  4. Age 19The first expedition. Bed 4 is found by accident, while looking for something else.
  5. Year oneThe dairy is leased. The library opens with three earths and one sieve.
  6. Collection 01Eleven pieces. Four survive. The catalogue begins.
  7. Collection 04The curing ground is established, at 2,940 metres, on a shelf nobody else wanted.
  8. Collection 07The Long Dry. Seven pieces; one returned to the earth in September.

We make one thing. We make it slowly.
