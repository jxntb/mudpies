007

No. 007 · Series: Fracture

Fault Line

Raked in a single unbroken spiral, thirty-one turns from rim to centre, then left in full sun until it disagreed with itself. The fissure opened on the nineteenth day, from the rim inward, and stopped a finger’s width from the centre, which the studio regards as good manners.

We considered filling it. We considered it for about a minute.

011

No. 011 · Series: Vessel

Meridian

A rolled lip of darker earth holds a field of pale grey silt, like a coastline drawn around a very calm sea. Two fractures cross the meridian at an angle the studio has measured, recorded, and chosen not to publish.

Meridian is the most conventionally useful piece in the collection, in that it could, in principle, hold something. We would ask that it doesn’t.

014

No. 014 · Series: Weather

Old Weather

Left uncovered through a week of indecisive weather: sun, then mist, then a single night of hail that the forecast had described as unlikely. The surface records all three. The pale streaks are not decoration; they are where the hail landed.

The edge was not shaped. It was allowed to happen.

019

No. 019 · Series: Fracture

Noon

Pressed at exactly midday and cured entirely in shade, as an experiment in patience. A hairline crack runs north to south and has not moved in four months. It is inspected every morning. It appears to know.

Reserved by a collector who asked, very reasonably, whether it would crack further, and who accepted our answer, which was that it might.

023

No. 023 · Series: Burnished

Nocturne

Burnished by hand with a river stone for nine consecutive evenings, until the surface took on the low shine of something that has been held for a very long time. A single chip, at ten o’clock, occurred during the ninth evening. The burnishing stopped there.

Best seen in low light. Best not seen in rain.

026

No. 026 · Series: Vessel

Salt Margin

A rim of red clay around a pale, wind-dried interior. The crack network appeared at altitude, in a single afternoon, and resembles — depending on who is asked — a river delta, a family tree, or the route home.

The last piece to come down from the curing ground. It took two people and most of a morning.

No. 031 · Series: Weather

Low Tide

Lost to the first rain of autumn on the fourteenth of September, two days before release. The lower half went first. What remains is photographed here as it was found: half a pie, half a puddle, entirely itself.

The studio considers it among its finest work, and is aware of how that sounds.
